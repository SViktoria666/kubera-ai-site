# Anthropic's Supported Regions Policy: What Changed on 12 November and How to Check Your Own Position

For most European mid-size companies, nothing about their access to Claude changed on 12 November 2026. On the pages we read on 16 November 2026, all 27 EU member states appear on Anthropic's list of supported countries, alongside the United Kingdom, Switzerland and Norway. What changed is how the rule is written. The [Supported Regions Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/supported-regions) now says in plain terms that eligibility follows three things: where a person is physically located, where a company is incorporated or headquartered, and who owns or controls it.

That matters because a company's access to an AI tool is no longer decided only by its own address. A laptop on a business trip, a subsidiary, a contractor team or a new investor can each move a user outside the line, and nobody has to decide to break a rule for that to happen. These are ordinary events, and each can be handled with a short written check, reviewed when something changes.

This article reads the policy text, separates what it states from what it leaves open, and offers a four-layer check you can run in an afternoon. It reflects the pages as read on 16 November 2026; the live page is the reference.

This article is not legal advice. Terms of service, ownership structures and sanctions-adjacent questions depend on your facts, so take legal advice for your own situation.

## What the policy says

The Supported Regions Policy states that Anthropic's products are available only in the countries and regions on its list. It names the apps, such as Claude.ai, Claude Code and Cowork, the API and developer platform, and Claude models offered through cloud platforms and authorized resellers. Territories and dependencies of a listed country are also supported, and the policy gives Puerto Rico as an example. The rule that does the work is one sentence: “Any country or region not listed is unsupported.”

The policy then names three kinds of use that fall under that sentence.

| Category | Who is covered | Does the individual's own location matter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical location | Anyone using the products while physically in an unsupported region, including users and personnel of companies that are otherwise supported | Yes, it is the test |
| Incorporation or headquarters | Companies incorporated or headquartered in an unsupported region, and their users and personnel | No, the policy applies regardless of where the individual sits |
| Ownership or control | Companies majority-owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by persons or entities in unsupported regions, and their users and personnel | No, the same wording applies |

In plain words, three consequences follow.

- A supported company does not carry its eligibility with its employees. The first category covers personnel of otherwise supported companies, so a person on a trip to a country outside the list is outside the policy while there.
- A company's own location can exclude people who sit in a listed country. Under the second and third categories, personnel are covered regardless of where they are physically located.
- Control reaches through structures. The words “directly or indirectly” mean that a holding company or a fund between you and an owner does not by itself change the question.

## What is new and what is not

The company-level rule is not new. The timeline is short, and each step comes from Anthropic.

| Date | Event | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 September 2025 | Anthropic announced it would bar companies whose ownership structures subject them to control from jurisdictions where its products are not permitted, and said this includes entities more than 50 percent owned, directly or indirectly, by companies headquartered in such regions | [Anthropic announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions) |
| Autumn 2026 | Anthropic published its 2026 Usage Policy update, saying it had updated the Supported Regions page to clarify how the policy is enforced | [Anthropic announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update) |
| 12 November 2026 | The updated Usage Policy and the Supported Regions Policy took effect | [Anthropic policy pages](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/supported-regions) |

Two details are worth keeping. First, the 2025 announcement used “includes” when it gave the 50 percent figure, so it already treated majority ownership as an example of control and not as the whole definition. The 2026 policy page uses “majority-owned or controlled” and does not define control further. Second, Anthropic's reason, as stated in 2025, is legal, regulatory and security risk. The policy is presented as a terms-of-service matter, not as a judgement about any company or person.

For a company inside the list, the practical change is that the physical-location test and the personnel wording are now written out. Anthropic describes the 2026 update as a clarification of enforcement. Either way, the checks below are the same.

## What the policy text does not tell you

The useful part of reading a policy is noticing what it leaves out. On the policy page itself, these points were not answered. Where Anthropic's terms add something, the table says so.

| Question | What we found |
| --- | --- |
| What happens after a breach | Not described on the policy page. The Usage Policy says that if Anthropic suspects a violation it may warn, throttle, limit, suspend or terminate access. Anthropic's Transparency Hub names the Supported Regions Policy among the policies it enforces in this way. These are rights Anthropic holds, not an automatic shutdown |
| Notice of a suspension | Not stated on the policy page. The Commercial Terms say Anthropic will use reasonable efforts to give written notice of a suspension and set no fixed period |
| Route to appeal | Not stated on the policy page. The Consumer Terms point to Anthropic's Trust and Safety Support Center for how to appeal a suspension or termination |
| How location is determined | Not stated. VPNs are not mentioned |
| What control means beyond majority ownership | Not defined on the policy page |
| How the rule applies to end users of products built on Claude | Not explained. The Usage Policy lists providing others, including end users, with access in violation of the Supported Regions Policy as a prohibited act |
| Which version of the list is current | Pages differ, see below |

The last point deserves a note. On the day we read them, Anthropic's [developer documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/supported-regions) carried a country list that was shorter than the list on the policy page, and it described the exclusions inside Ukraine differently: three regions in the documentation, five on the policy page. The documentation page carries no date and does not link to the policy, so we cannot say how the two relate. Treat the policy page, which carries the effective date of 12 November 2026 and a version history link, as the reference, and write down the date you read it. We did not read the version history itself.

The terms give Anthropic a right to suspend where it reasonably believes there is a violation. That is a right, not an automatic shutdown, and the Commercial Terms promise reasonable efforts at notice, not a notice period. A company that depends on one provider should still have a plan, covered below.

## The Kubera Four-Layer Eligibility Check

Kubera AI planning heuristic, not a universal benchmark. It is not a validated method.

The check turns the three categories into four questions a company can answer from records it already holds. Layer four, the route, is added because the same policy applies whether you reach Claude directly, through a cloud platform or through a reseller.

| Layer | Question to answer | Evidence to keep | Suggested owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Place | In which countries do people who use Claude physically work, including trips, remote work and holidays | Staff location list, travel calendar, remote work approvals | HR with IT |
| Entity | Where is each company in the group, and each contractor company, incorporated or headquartered | Group structure chart, registry extracts for contractors | Finance or legal |
| Control | Who owns or controls the group, directly or indirectly, above a threshold you set for review | Shareholder register, investor agreements, beneficial owner records | Legal |
| Route | How do people reach Claude: apps, API keys, a cloud platform, a reseller, or a product you run for clients | Inventory of accounts, keys and integrations with a named owner each | IT |

Most companies will find that Place is the layer with real movement, because people travel. Entity and Control change slowly, but a surprise there would affect many users at once, so they deserve a look even when you expect a clean answer. The evidence column matters: the [Amazon Bedrock terms](https://aws.amazon.com/legal/bedrock/third-party-models/) we read require customers to cooperate with reasonable requests from Anthropic to verify their identity and use of the services, and records make that a short task.

## Where exposure shows up in a mid-size company

### Travel, remote work and personal accounts

The first category ties eligibility to physical location. A sales manager who opens Claude on a hotel network in a country outside the list is, by the policy's wording, using the product from an unsupported region, even if the company is fully eligible. Developers are a particular case: a laptop with a coding agent installed, such as Claude Code, goes where the developer goes, and so does an API key stored in its environment. For how coding agents compare, see [Claude Code vs OpenAI Codex](/blog/claude-code-vs-openai-codex).

Personal accounts on work trips are the old [shadow AI risk](/blog/shadow-ai-business-risk-europe) in a new form. An employee who cannot use the company account while travelling may reach for a personal one, and then the company has no record of what was used or where. We describe that pattern in shadow AI risk.

A workable travel rule is short. Before a trip, the traveller checks the destination against the current list. If it is not listed, they use a company-approved alternative available there, or work without AI tools. Whichever alternative you name, check that provider's own terms for the destination first. We did not compare providers' lists for this article.

### Subsidiaries and contractor companies

The second category applies to an entity that is incorporated or headquartered in an unsupported region, and to its personnel, wherever those people sit. A group with a subsidiary or a delivery partner in a country outside the list should ask whether that entity's staff are using accounts, keys or seats that belong to the group. The policy does not say how Anthropic treats an individual freelancer who lives in a listed country and works for you as a person, not through a company. Put that question to Anthropic or to your counsel.

### Owners and investors

The third category covers companies majority-owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, from unsupported regions. For a closely held mid-size company, the shareholder register usually answers the question in ten minutes. Where it does not, because of nominee holdings, funds or a pending round, ask for advice before the transaction closes. Because the policy does not define control beyond ownership, a minority holder with strong rights is the case to raise with counsel.

### Products and services you run for others

If your company builds a customer-facing assistant on Claude, or an agency runs automations on a client's behalf, the question widens. The Usage Policy lists providing others, including end users, with access in violation of the Supported Regions Policy as a prohibited act. The pages we read do not explain how this applies to the end users of a public chatbot, for example a customer who writes to it from a holiday abroad. Do not assume an answer in either direction. Ask your Anthropic contact or reseller, get the answer in writing, and record it.

The cloud route does not change eligibility

The policy page says it covers Claude models offered through cloud platforms and authorized resellers, and the cloud terms say the same in contract language. In the Anthropic terms hosted by AWS for Amazon Bedrock, version dated 18 August 2025, customers and their users may use the services only in compliance with the Supported Regions Policy, and only Anthropic is named as able to suspend, where it reasonably believes there is a violation, with reasonable efforts to give written notice. Google Cloud's [Service Specific Terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms) require Anthropic models to be used in compliance with the Supported Regions Policy, and let Google suspend or terminate access to third-party models immediately if it reasonably suspects a violation of the model terms. So moving from a direct account to a cloud provider does not remove the question. It changes who holds the right to suspend. We did not read the Microsoft Foundry terms, and all of these terms change, so check the version in your contract.

Eligibility is also separate from the data question. Where your data is processed is a different decision, covered in [AI data safety for European businesses](/blog/ai-data-safety-european-businesses).

## Five steps for this week

1. **Save the policy and note the date.** Download the Supported Regions Policy page, record the effective date and the date you read it, and note the version history link. Assign one person to check it monthly.
2. **Build the Place register.** List the countries where staff who use Claude work, including approved remote work, and attach the travel rule from above. Ask people to check the destination before they travel.
3. **Draw the group and contractor chart.** Mark each company's place of incorporation and headquarters. Mark which contractor companies use your accounts or keys.
4. **Inventory accounts and keys.** For every account, API key and integration that reaches Claude, name an owner and the route. Remove shared personal accounts from work use.
5. **Write the open questions and send them.** Typical ones: how control is defined beyond majority ownership, how the rule applies to end users of your products, and what notice and appeal route apply to your contract. Keep the answers with the policy copy.

## Continuity without alarm

The terms promise reasonable efforts at notice, not a fixed period, so a company that depends on one provider for daily work should know what it would do the day after access stopped. Keep the plan small. List the three or four workflows that depend on Claude, name a second provider or model that could cover each, and test the swap once on a non-critical task. Open-weights models may be part of the answer for some workflows, but their licences differ, and we explain how to read them in [Is it really open source](/blog/is-it-really-open-source-ai-agent-licence-check). ## Illustrative scenario

Illustrative scenario, not a specific Kubera client. We describe the design intent here, not a measured result.

A 90-person logistics software company in the Netherlands, with all details invented. It has a sales team that travels, a small delivery team run by a contractor company, and one minority investor holding a stake through a fund. The managing director runs the four-layer check in an afternoon.

Place: the sales team's trips are mostly within the list, but a conference is planned in a country that is not on it. The company adds the travel rule, and the traveller will use the company-approved alternative for that week. Entity: the group sits in a listed country, but the contractor company is incorporated elsewhere, so the managing director finds out which accounts its staff use. Control: the investor's stake is a minority, but it carries board rights, so the company writes the question down for counsel before the next funding round. Route: the check finds two API keys that nobody owns and one personal account used for client demos. All three are removed or reassigned.

The design intent is a one-page register, a travel rule and a short list of questions for the provider, each with an owner and a date.

## What not to do

- **Do not mask a location.** The policy is written around where a person is physically located. A tool that hides the location does not change where the person is, and the entity and control layers are not affected by it at all. The policy text does not mention VPNs, but it does not need to for this reasoning to hold.
- **Do not assume a cloud account is a workaround.** The policy covers cloud platforms and resellers.
- **Do not react by banning AI tools.** The risk comes from unmanaged use. A register, a travel rule and a named owner address it, and your teams keep the benefit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the policy apply to a company based in the EU?

Yes, to the extent that its use falls under one of the three categories. All 27 EU member states appeared on the list on the pages we read on 16 November 2026, so a company based in one is generally inside the list. The policy still applies to people travelling outside it, to entities incorporated or headquartered elsewhere, and to ownership or control from unsupported regions.

### Can employees use Claude on a business trip or holiday?

It depends on where they are. The policy ties eligibility to physical location and covers personnel of otherwise supported companies. If the destination is on the list, there is no issue under that category. If it is not, the use is outside the policy while the person is there. Check the current list before travel.

### What if we have a subsidiary or contractor company in a country that is not listed?

The second category covers companies incorporated or headquartered in an unsupported region and their users and personnel, regardless of where those individuals sit. Find out whether that entity's staff use accounts, keys or seats that belong to your group, and take advice on any arrangement where they do.

### What does “majority-owned or controlled” mean for our investors?

Majority ownership is the clearest case. Anthropic's 2025 announcement gave more than 50 percent, held directly or indirectly, as an example of control. The 2026 policy page adds the word controlled and does not define it further. If an investor holds a minority stake with strong rights, ask your counsel and put the question to Anthropic.

### Does using Claude through a cloud platform change anything?

Not for eligibility. The policy states that it covers Claude models offered through cloud platforms and authorized resellers. The Amazon Bedrock terms we read, version dated 18 August 2025, and Google Cloud's Service Specific Terms both require compliance with the Supported Regions Policy and give the provider or Anthropic a right to suspend where a violation is suspected. We did not read the Microsoft Foundry terms.

### What happens if we get it wrong?

The policy page does not describe consequences. The Usage Policy says Anthropic may warn, throttle, limit, suspend or terminate access where it suspects a violation, and the Transparency Hub lists the Supported Regions Policy among the policies it enforces. These are rights Anthropic holds, not an automatic shutdown. The Commercial Terms promise reasonable efforts at written notice of a suspension but no fixed period, and the Consumer Terms point to a support center for appeals, so a continuity plan is sensible.

### Are our customers covered if they use an assistant we built on Claude?

The pages we read do not answer this. The Usage Policy lists giving others, including end users, access in violation of the Supported Regions Policy as a prohibited act, but it does not explain how that applies to a public chatbot. Ask Anthropic or your reseller and get the answer in writing.

### Is a VPN a solution for travellers?

No. The policy is written around physical location, and a VPN changes none of the other layers. It also leaves the company without a record of what was used where. A company-approved alternative is the cleaner route.

### Where do I find the current list of countries?

On Anthropic's [Supported Regions Policy page](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/supported-regions), which shows the effective date and a link to version history. On the day we read the pages, the developer documentation list was undated and differed from the policy page list, so use the policy page and record the date you read it.

### Should we stop using Claude?

There is no reason to stop on the basis of this policy if your people, entities and owners are inside the list. The sensible response is a register, a travel rule, an owner for every account and key, and a small continuity plan. That keeps the benefit of the tool and removes the surprises.
