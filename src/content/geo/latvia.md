Business Challenges Specific to the Latvian Market
1. Workforce Shrinkage and Labour Shortages
Latvia faces one of Europe's most acute demographic challenges. EURES Latvia (2024) confirms: as older workers retire, insufficient younger workers are entering the market — particularly in healthcare, education, construction, and manufacturing. The economic consequence is clear: automation is increasingly necessary to maintain operational capacity as the workforce shrinks.
2. AI Adoption Growing Rapidly — Early Movers Capture Advantage
Latvia's AI adoption is accelerating rapidly. According to the Cybernews AI Adoption Index 2025, Latvia's individual AI adoption reached 55% — placing Latvia 4th globally. Enterprise-level adoption is following this trajectory. Companies that build AI-automated operations now establish advantages before competitors catch up.
3. SME-Dominated Economy
93% of Latvian enterprises are micro-companies with fewer than 10 employees (EURES Latvia 2024). These companies need operational leverage. Automation enables lean Latvian teams to serve more clients, respond faster, and manage more complex pipelines than their headcount alone would allow.
4. Baltic and Nordic Market Expansion
Latvian companies increasingly operate across Estonia, Lithuania, and Nordic markets. Managing multilingual Baltic communications — Latvian, Estonian, Lithuanian, Russian, English — manually is operationally demanding. AI handles it systematically.
5. Rising Labour Costs
Latvia's wages have risen substantially. The competitive low-cost labour advantage that once defined the Latvian market is narrowing. Automation compensates for rising employment costs by increasing output per employee rather than increasing headcount.
