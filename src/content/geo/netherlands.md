Business Challenges Specific to the Dutch Market
1. Persistent Labour Shortages
According to Statistics Netherlands (CBS), two-thirds of Dutch businesses — 67% of medium-sized companies — are currently dealing with staff shortages. Research by UWV (the Employee Insurance Agency) found that Dutch employers struggled to fill 45% of vacancies in 2025. With 97 job openings for every 100 unemployed people at the end of 2025 (De Nederlandsche Bank), the Dutch labour market remains fundamentally tight. AI automation is how ambitious companies extend their operational capacity without waiting for the talent market to loosen.
2. High Labour Costs Pressure Margins
The Netherlands has some of Western Europe's highest employment costs — the minimum wage alone reached €2,193 per month in January 2025. Every hour a skilled employee spends on repetitive administrative work is expensive capacity wasted on tasks that a system can handle.
3. Digital Adoption Gap Among SMEs
The OECD's 2025 Economic Survey of the Netherlands found that digital adoption rates remain uneven, with SMEs lagging significantly behind larger firms. Labour shortages in ICT fields and insufficient digital skills further limit the ability of smaller companies to integrate automation tools — creating a gap that Kubera AI is designed to close: we do the technical integration, so your team doesn't need to.
4. Internationalization Without Operational Infrastructure
Dutch companies routinely operate across multiple European markets — often in Dutch, English, German, and French simultaneously. Managing leads, communications, and support across multiple languages manually is unsustainable. Multilingual automation handles it without proportionally growing the team.
5. CRM Underutilization Causing Revenue Leakage
Despite high software adoption rates, many Dutch SMEs have CRM systems (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive) that are poorly maintained. Reps don't log calls. Deals are tracked in email threads. Follow-ups are forgotten. Revenue leakage from unmanaged pipeline is invisible until it's too late — and entirely preventable with automation.
